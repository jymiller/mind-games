Deal Memory · Trust, Audit & Identity

Can Enid prove who changed what — and replay it?

Our capability tests proved Enid can remember and correct. They said nothing about trust. This is the honest picture — grounded in a live probe of the API — of what XTrace records, what it doesn't, and what you build yourself.

Short answer: the system remembers what a number was and when it changed — but not, reliably, who changed it, and a deleted memory leaves no trace.

So value-level traceability is real and native. The who and the immutable, tamper-evident parts — the ones a regulator cares about — you add in your own layer. Below is exactly where the line falls.

The division of labor

XTrace is a memory, not a system of record. It tracks how beliefs change; it does not seal them against tampering or reliably stamp who made each change. That second job is yours — and the wet-ink chassis already does it.

The three gaps to design around

None of these are dealbreakers — they're the reason the immutable ledger lives in your layer, not XTrace's. All three are confirmed on the live API.

No system-wide audit log

There's no "everything that happened" feed — only the per-fact revision chain. You reconstruct the timeline yourself.

probed: /audit · /events · /activity · /logs · /memories/{id}/history → all 404
Author attribution isn't reliable

After Bob corrected Jane's fact, the old version came back attributed to Bob. The chain is trustworthy for values and times — not for "who." Stamp the actor yourself, at write time.

probed: superseded node returned agent = officer-bob, not officer-jane
Delete is permanent

Removing a memory erases it outright — no tombstone, no trace. So XTrace alone is erasable, not tamper-evident. Your append-only ledger is what makes the record survive.

probed: after delete, get → 404 not found (only group archive is a soft flag)

The old values are simply gone

6 current facts carry a pointer that says "I replaced an earlier value" — and all 6 of those earlier values are absent from the export. The pointer dangles into nothing.

e.g. deal-audit: "leverage is 4.0× (supersedes 8d6f…)" — but 8d6f (the 3.5×, written by Jane) isn't in the file

The instant a number is corrected, its prior value drops out of the current view — and out of any export. The before-and-after survives only in the live per-fact chain (where, remember, the "who" is unreliable). This is the clearest proof yet that the replayable, immutable record has to be yours.

Not one brain — roles & boundaries

Memory isn't a single undifferentiated pool. Six identity axes let you model who and what a memory belongs to — so a deal team, individual officers, and the firm can share the right slice and keep the rest private.

user_idThe deal. The memory namespace — NRA-1's world. Corrections supersede within it.
agent_idThe officer / role. Jane vs Bob vs the agent itself.
app_idThe firm. A shared, firm-wide knowledge base that outlives any analyst.
conv_idOne session. A single quarter's review.
group_idsA shared pool. The deal team — how one officer's covenant reaches another.
namespaceA working context. Scopes learned rules to where they apply.

Private vs global: memories judged personal stay with their owner and are never shared; only shareable ones get tagged to a group or the firm store. The boundary is built in — you just have to write with the right identity.
